H1 ★ — Buried-fault gravity edges TOP CANDIDATE

Layers: isostatic gravity anomaly + slope of isostatic gravity (official bands, provided features). Deep arm adds an upward-continued variant of the same band.

Physical signature: zero-contour of the tilt derivative (TDR) + horizontal-gradient magnitude (HGM), computed spectrally (d/dx→ikx, d/dy→iky, d/dz→|k|). The TDR zero contour tracks lateral edges of density units independent of anomaly amplitude; HGM peaks over the same edges. Emission = skeletonised zero-contour proximity weighted by normalised HGM. Implemented in src/gems/features.py (tilt_derivative, horizontal_gradient_magnitude, zero_contour_proximity, skeleton_thin).

Why it catches unmapped faults: the USGS Quaternary compilation maps faults with surface expression (scarps, offset layers — see the about page: how geologists detect faults). Buried range-front faults under basin fill have no surface scarp but juxtapose dense basement against light fill — a gravity edge. The USGS Nevada gravity memo states explicitly that isostatic-anomaly patterns "reveal features of subsurface geology, including the location of buried faults, sedimentary basins, plutons, uplifted basement rocks" (USGS DS 234). The experts' new-fault set was compiled to complete the catalogue — buried structures are exactly the population a surface catalogue under-samples.

How it differs from implemented work: 6GEMSDOE computed HGM/analytic signal/tilt on magnetic bands inside an HGB blender; GEMSDOE4 built Sato ridgeness + structure-tensor coherence on edge bands generally. No group line isolates the gravity-only TDR-zero-contour family, no line runs the upward-continued deep arm, and no line emits skeletonised contour proximity as its primary field rather than as one blender input among dozens.

Validation: python scripts/holdout_validate.py — H1 vs a DEM-only arm on 4×4 blocked folds with 300 m buffers; selection on the proxy (unmapped stand-in) DTI with known-fault masking reported both ways. Real-band run PENDING data/ placement; fixture run passes (see Results).

H2 — Actively dilating strain corridors

Layers: dilatation rate, shear strain rate, second invariant of the strain-rate tensor (official bands). Reference science: UNR Nevada Geodetic Laboratory strain-rate model of the Pacific–North America plate boundary (UNR), Kreemer et al. NBMG Map 178.

Physical signature: normalised indices — dilation = dilatation/(II+ε), shear-partitioning = |shear|/(II+ε) — thresholded to corridors, then skeletonised. Dilation highs mark extensional stepovers/relay zones where crust is opening now and fluids ascend.

Why unmapped: the catalogue records discrete traces; distributed deforming relays between traces have no single mappable line yet carry the geothermal signal (faults as fluid conduits — about page). INGENIOUS itself ships slip/dilation-tendency grids for the same reason (dataset listing).

Novelty: siblings feed raw strain bands to blenders; none builds an explicit dilation-tendency corridor field or tests the distributed-vs-discrete hypothesis on the holdout.

H3 — Asymmetric scarp steps, calibrated on 1 m DEM

Layers: detrended elevation + slope (100 m competition bands) for inference; 1 m 3DEP DEM tiles for calibration only.

Physical signature: the antisymmetric step response along the local gradient direction (mean upslope − mean downslope over ±2 px), plus break-in-slope and Zevenbergen–Thorne profile curvature as support. A scarp is a STEP; a ridge or valley is symmetric — this separates scarps from the generic ridgeness every sibling lineament detector fires on.

Why unmapped: subtle (<2 m) scarps wash out at 100 m but are visible in 1 m lidar DEM — the same data the competition explicitly ships links for (1m_DEM_links.csv) and the same GeoDAWN lidar the experts plausibly used (new-fault identification sources questioned in forum 11527). Calibrating the 100 m step threshold against 1 m-measured scarps transfers lidar-grade truth to grid scale.

Novelty: no sibling implements an antisymmetric step filter; GEMSDOE4's Sato/structure-tensor detectors are symmetric lineament measures. No sibling calibrates against the 1 m tiles.

External data (verified obtainable, free): USGS 3DEP 1 m DEM — (a) the competition's own 1m_DEM_links.csv; fallback (b) TNM staged products S3 browser and (c) the AWS public dataset s3://usgs-lidar-public (EPT, free) / s3://usgs-lidar (Requester Pays full-density) (hobuinc/usgs-lidar). All public domain.

H4 — Conductive alteration lineaments

Layers: surface conductivity + depth to conductive base (official bands; MT-derived conductance in the INGENIOUS compilation).

Physical signature: HGM of surface conductivity (lateral alteration boundaries) × HGM of depth-to-base (cover steps); emit where both change together (conductivity_edges).

Why unmapped: fault gouge and hydrothermal clay caps are conductive without necessarily offsetting magnetic units or breaking the surface — invisible to magnetics-first and scarp-first detectors, hence to catalogue compilers.

Novelty: no sibling line isolates the EM/conductivity family at all.

H5 — Seismicity ∩ shallow-basement intersections

Layers: earthquake density + top-of-crustal magnetic source depth (official bands).

Physical signature: quantile intersection — top-decile earthquake density where magnetic basement is shallowest-quartile (blind_fault_intersection). Microseismic lineaments along a buried structure with an uplifted basement block = blind active fault.

Why unmapped: blind faults by definition lack surface expression; the Quaternary catalogue requires geologic evidence of surface deformation (USGS faults page). Seismicity is the only surface-observable witness.

Novelty: siblings use earthquake density as a raw blender band; none intersects it with basement depth as a blind-fault test.

Validation protocol (binding)

  1. Spatial blocks + buffer: 4×4 blocks, 300 m (3 px) train-exclusion buffer — no train pixel inside the metric kernel of test truth (src/gems/holdout.py).
  2. Two truths reported: catalogue DTI (monitor, wrong population) and proxy DTI on unmapped stand-ins (selection signal). Known-fault masking reported both unmasked and masked (exact + dilated-3px) so the masking assumption is measured, not hidden.
  3. Decision rule: a hypothesis spends a weekly slot only if it beats the current holdout best on proxy DTI. Current best: none — no real-band run yet.
  4. Emission: binary {0,1} skeleton fields; the metric is monotone in emitted value, so fractional confidence gives score away (verified algebra in src/gems/metric.py docstring logic; top-k budgeting in holdout.topk_field).
