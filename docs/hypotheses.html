H1 ★ — Buried-fault gravity edges TOP CANDIDATE

Layers: isostatic gravity anomaly + slope of isostatic gravity (official bands, provided features). Deep arm adds an upward-continued variant of the same band.

Physical signature: zero-contour of the tilt derivative (TDR) + horizontal-gradient magnitude (HGM), computed spectrally (d/dx→ikx, d/dy→iky, d/dz→|k|). The TDR zero contour tracks lateral edges of density units independent of anomaly amplitude; HGM peaks over the same edges. Emission = skeletonised zero-contour proximity weighted by normalised HGM. Implemented in src/gems/features.py (tilt_derivative, horizontal_gradient_magnitude, zero_contour_proximity, skeleton_thin).

Why it catches unmapped faults: the USGS Quaternary compilation maps faults with surface expression (scarps, offset layers — see the about page: how geologists detect faults). Buried range-front faults under basin fill have no surface scarp but juxtapose dense basement against light fill — a gravity edge. The USGS Nevada gravity memo states explicitly that isostatic-anomaly patterns "reveal features of subsurface geology, including the location of buried faults, sedimentary basins, plutons, uplifted basement rocks" (USGS DS 234). The experts' new-fault set was compiled to complete the catalogue — buried structures are exactly the population a surface catalogue under-samples.

How it differs from implemented work: 6GEMSDOE computed HGM/analytic signal/tilt on magnetic bands inside an HGB blender; GEMSDOE4 built Sato ridgeness + structure-tensor coherence on edge bands generally. No group line isolates the gravity-only TDR-zero-contour family, no line runs the upward-continued deep arm, and no line emits skeletonised contour proximity as its primary field rather than as one blender input among dozens.

Validation (MEASURED 2026-09-26): fixture run passes — H1-spectral 0.84 vs DEM-only 0.07 on a synthetic buried fault (operator works when signal is present). Real bands (scripts/holdout_real.py, provided-gradient TDR + two variants): catalogue-fold DTI 0.038 (H1b 0.044, H1c 0.042) vs random 0.170; proxy-fold DTI 0.010–0.018 vs random 0.171. Falsified as a catalogue detector — which is expected, since it targets the catalogue-complementary buried population by design. Untested as a buried-fault detector: no buried-fault truth exists. NO SLOT — second-slot contrast candidate only.

H2 — Actively dilating strain corridors

Layers: dilatation rate, shear strain rate, second invariant of the strain-rate tensor (official bands). Reference science: UNR Nevada Geodetic Laboratory strain-rate model of the Pacific–North America plate boundary (UNR), Kreemer et al. NBMG Map 178.

Physical signature: normalised indices — dilation = dilatation/(II+ε), shear-partitioning = |shear|/(II+ε) — thresholded to corridors, then skeletonised. Dilation highs mark extensional stepovers/relay zones where crust is opening now and fluids ascend.

Why unmapped: the catalogue records discrete traces; distributed deforming relays between traces have no single mappable line yet carry the geothermal signal (faults as fluid conduits — about page). INGENIOUS itself ships slip/dilation-tendency grids for the same reason (dataset listing).

Novelty: siblings feed raw strain bands to blenders; none builds an explicit dilation-tendency corridor field or tests the distributed-vs-discrete hypothesis on the holdout.

Validation (MEASURED 2026-09-26): catalogue 0.011 (fold 0: 0.042, two folds exactly 0.000), proxy 0.014 vs random 0.171. Concentrated corridor mass is structurally punished by fold-DTI (mass in train regions scores ~0 on test folds), so this number measures geographic concentration more than geological merit — fault-system folds are the fairer test (not yet implemented). NO SLOT.

H3 — Asymmetric scarp steps, calibrated on 1 m DEM

Layers: detrended elevation + slope (100 m competition bands) for inference; 1 m 3DEP DEM tiles for calibration only.

Physical signature: the antisymmetric step response along the local gradient direction (mean upslope − mean downslope over ±2 px), plus break-in-slope and Zevenbergen–Thorne profile curvature as support. A scarp is a STEP; a ridge or valley is symmetric — this separates scarps from the generic ridgeness every sibling lineament detector fires on.

Why unmapped: subtle (<2 m) scarps wash out at 100 m but are visible in 1 m lidar DEM — the same data the competition explicitly ships links for (1m_DEM_links.csv) and the same GeoDAWN lidar the experts plausibly used (new-fault identification sources questioned in forum 11527). Calibrating the 100 m step threshold against 1 m-measured scarps transfers lidar-grade truth to grid scale.

Novelty: no sibling implements an antisymmetric step filter; GEMSDOE4's Sato/structure-tensor detectors are symmetric lineament measures. No sibling calibrates against the 1 m tiles.

External data (verified obtainable, free): USGS 3DEP 1 m DEM — (a) the competition's own 1m_DEM_links.csv; fallback (b) TNM staged products S3 browser and (c) the AWS public dataset s3://usgs-lidar-public (EPT, free) / s3://usgs-lidar (Requester Pays full-density) (hobuinc/usgs-lidar). All public domain.

Validation (MEASURED 2026-09-26): catalogue 0.039, proxy 0.127 — the former best arm on either population (H9 now leads at 0.142), still below random 0.171. The weak 100 m-only catalogue number supports H3's own premise (100 m is too coarse without 1 m calibration). First far-field contrast candidate if the apex slot shows near-catalogue mass is not rewarded. NO SLOT yet.

H4 — Conductive alteration lineaments

Layers: surface conductivity + depth to conductive base (official bands; MT-derived conductance in the INGENIOUS compilation).

Physical signature: HGM of surface conductivity (lateral alteration boundaries) × HGM of depth-to-base (cover steps); emit where both change together (conductivity_edges).

Why unmapped: fault gouge and hydrothermal clay caps are conductive without necessarily offsetting magnetic units or breaking the surface — invisible to magnetics-first and scarp-first detectors, hence to catalogue compilers.

Novelty: no sibling line isolates the EM/conductivity family at all.

Caveat: the text's "depth to conductive base" has no exact tag match; band 15 ("depth to basement … sediment thickness") is used instead (Sources F9).

Validation (MEASURED 2026-09-26): catalogue 0.033, proxy 0.018 vs random 0.171. NO SLOT.

H5 — Seismicity ∩ shallow-basement intersections REDEFINED 2026-09-26

Layers: earthquake density (ieq_n100a15, band 16) ∩ shallow sediment-basement depth (depth_to_base_surf, band 15). The originally specified "top-of-crustal magnetic source depth" band DOES NOT EXIST in the file despite being listed in the problem text — verified from the 19 band_name tags (Sources F9; tests/test_bandlayout.py fails loudly if a future data revision adds one).

Physical signature: quantile intersection — top-decile earthquake density where basement is shallowest-quartile (blind_fault_intersection). Microseismic lineaments along a buried structure with an uplifted basement block = blind active fault.

Why unmapped: blind faults by definition lack surface expression; the Quaternary catalogue requires geologic evidence of surface deformation (USGS faults page). Seismicity is the only surface-observable witness.

Novelty: siblings use earthquake density as a raw blender band; none intersects it with basement depth as a blind-fault test.

Validation (MEASURED 2026-09-26): catalogue 0.026, proxy 0.025 vs random 0.171 — and the highest fold variance of any arm (fold 0: 0.055, fold 1: 0.005), i.e. unstable. NO SLOT.

H6 — Hedge composite (2% near + 0.5% H1 + 0.5% H3)

Motivation: the hidden truth's population split (new geometry of known systems vs entirely concealed faults) is unknown, so H6 buys both: 2% of the pixel budget on the near-catalogue structural ranking plus 0.5% each on the two most defensible far-field arms.

Validation (MEASURED 2026-09-26, scripts/holdout_hedge.py): composite (mean of catalogue/proxy fold-means) 0.429 vs apex-topk03 0.451 vs random 0.171 — the in-sample catalogue half dominates the composite, so this test cannot validate the hedge either. Concept retained for the conditional second slot (see Results decision tree). NO SLOT on composite evidence.

H7 — Magnetic basement lineaments (TDR × analytic signal)

Layers: TMI horizontal gradient + TMI vertical gradient (official bands 3 and 9 — the survey's own published gradients).

Physical signature: tilt derivative TDR = atan2(vg, |hg|) with zero-contour proximity, weighted by normalised analytic-signal amplitude AS = sqrt(hg² + vg²) (magnetic_tdr_as). Elementwise — no FFT.

Why unmapped: "Gravity and magnetic surveys also help infer the presence of faults through density or magnetic anomalies" and many GeoDAWN faults "are hidden below the surface, requiring geophysical data to detect" (about page). Buried basement faults juxtapose units of contrasting magnetization with no surface expression, while the catalogue covers faults showing "geological evidence of coseismic surface deformation" (USGS faults page).

Novelty: H1 is gravity-only TDR; Apex uses Sobel(TMI-HG) only as one GBT input; no line emits the magnetic-only TDR-zero-contour × AS primary field.

Validation (MEASURED 2026-09-26): catalogue 0.047, proxy 0.041 vs random 0.170/0.171 — below random on both (same pattern as H1: edges avoid catalogue + proxy). Untested as a buried-fault detector. NO SLOT.

H8 ★ — Tip-to-tip relay-linkage corridors TOP NEW CANDIDATE

Layers: catalogue geometry (labels.tif) + dilatation rate (band 8) as the extension weight.

Physical signature: tips of DIFFERENT connected components 3–20 px apart whose outward strike vectors point at each other (mean cosine ≥ 0.5) are joined by a straight corridor weighted by gap decay × collinearity × mean positive dilatation (fault_endpoints, tip_linkage_corridors).

Why unmapped: relay/linkage faults between mapped segments are exactly "newly mapped geometry of an existing fault system", which the staff definition of "new fault" explicitly includes (forum 11516).

Novelty: Apex rays project from SINGLE tips and STOP at catalogue pixels — no inter-tip corridor is ever emitted; no dilatation/collinearity weighting exists anywhere in the programme.

Validation (MEASURED 2026-09-26, scripts/holdout_new.py): whole-component folds (truth = held-out components, predictions admitted on test ∪ buffer so the 300 m kernel credits near-hits): native 0.0001 vs matched random 0.0490 and rays 0.0045; padded-to-3% 0.2281 vs random 0.2580. Corridors enter eval zones (9–29 px/fold) but earn TP≈0–5 vs random TP≈500–680 — they connect train tips through regions away from held-out components. FALSIFIED on gap-bridging — NO SLOT.

H9 — Valley-axis alignment lineaments (map-view coherence)

Layers: detrended elevation (band 12).

Physical signature: structure-tensor orientation coherence of the elevation gradient field × normalised break-in-slope (valley_axis_coherence). Linear valleys / shutter ridges / deflected drainages are coherent; flats forced to 0 by an energy floor. Sign-free.

Why unmapped: linear valleys and drainage deflections persist after the scarp step erodes below 100 m detectability; map-view alignment is invisible to cross-scarp profile operators and to compilers mapping offset layers (about page).

Novelty: H3 samples an antisymmetric step ALONG the gradient; H9 measures orientation coherence IN MAP VIEW — same bands, orthogonal operator.

Validation (MEASURED 2026-09-26): catalogue 0.075 (best unsupervised catalogue arm), proxy 0.142 — the best unsupervised arm on either population (stable folds 0.123–0.166), still below random 0.171. New first far-field contrast candidate (replaces H3). NO SLOT yet.

H10 — Orientation-coherent microseismic ridges

Layers: earthquake density (band 16) + distance-to-quake (band 10 — used nowhere else in this repo).

Physical signature: smoothed earthquake-density ridge × structure-tensor coherence (linear ridges, not blobs) × proximity weight 1/(1+deq/median) (seismic_ridge_coherence).

Why unmapped: blind seismogenic faults lack surface expression and the catalogue requires surface-deformation evidence (USGS faults page); microseismic lineaments are the only surface-observable witness.

Novelty: H5 is a binary quantile intersection with basement (blob gating, no orientation); H10 is continuous, orientation-selective, basement-free.

Validation (MEASURED 2026-09-26): catalogue 0.048, proxy 0.023 vs random 0.171. NO SLOT.

Validation protocol (binding)

  1. Spatial blocks + buffer: 4×4 blocks, 300 m (3 px) train-exclusion buffer — no train pixel inside the metric kernel of test truth (src/gems/holdout.py).
  2. Two truths reported: catalogue DTI (monitor, wrong population) and proxy DTI on unmapped stand-ins (selection signal). Known-fault masking reported both unmasked and masked (exact + dilated-3px) so the masking assumption is measured, not hidden.
  3. Decision rule: a hypothesis spends a weekly slot only if it beats the current holdout best on proxy DTI. Current best: random@3% = 0.171 — no arm beats it.
  4. Emission: binary {0,1} top-k fields with EXACT budgets (a ≥-threshold rule over-selects 2–10× on plateau fields; holdout.topk_field uses an exact-k partition with deterministic tie-break). Measured, not proven: hardening at matched mass beats soft (0.8317 vs 0.7619) and a smaller budget beats a larger one (3%: 0.8907 vs 4.06%: 0.8317). An earlier "monotonicity proof" was wrong (binary is NOT optimal under the triangular kernel) and has been removed from the code.
