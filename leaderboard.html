Why GEMSDOE and 5GEMSDOE scored the same (0.1563)

Finding: same bytes, same score — upgraded to a TRIPLE duplicate by direct byte reads on 2026-09-26 (scripts/audit_siblings.py). Evidence:

Same pixel field ⇒ same DTI on any label set, so 0.1563 = 0.1563 is expected, not a coincidence. 5GEMSDOE additionally offers a "maximum-compatibility" variant (0.0 outside instead of NaN); under the official scorer both encode the same prediction, so it cannot score differently either. The SDCF9 account's second upload (0.1563, observed live 2026-09-26) extends the duplication to a third board row — same value, no new information. The 8GEMSDOE apex file (b83ea0e7…) matches none of the eleven audited sibling artifacts.

Consequence for this repo: uniqueness is enforced in code, not by convention — record_submission() refuses to log a payload whose hash already appears (override requires --allow-duplicate and writes a logged warning), and every build carries a unique timestamped filename plus a paste-ready Note.

Secondary lesson: the two uploads consumed two accounts' weekly slots for one measurement. Under the one-entity rule (rules §3.4/A.3 — one final submission per entity), parallel same-payload uploads are pure waste. 8GEMSDOE spends at most one slot per week, on the holdout winner only.

What the group scores teach (read carefully)

  • 0.156x cluster (GEMSDOE/5GEMSDOE/GEMSDOE2): dense catalogue-trained U-Net ensembles plateau together — the detector, not the emission, is the ceiling. GEMSDOE2's own page reaches the same conclusion ("if it scores at or below 0.1563 … the problem is the detector").
  • 0.1193 vs 0.1152 (nodes vs dense control): sparse placement beats dense ridges at fixed budget on the real board — supports skeletonised emission for all H1–H5 arms. (Mappings †; and note the inversion: on our SGMC proxy the dense ridge wins 0.30 vs 0.23 — the proxy may be backwards. SUGGESTIVE, not conclusive.)
  • 0.0830 (catalogue-gap arm): training only on catalogue-missing pixels underperformed — a warning that naive "unmapped-only" supervision hurts; our proxy protocol keeps catalogue supervision but selects on unmapped stand-ins.
  • 0.0286/0.0343 (operator-reported): if accurate, the HGB-top-3% and lineament-union lines severely underperform the U-Net plateau — consistent with a too-small or misplaced pixel budget, not necessarily with bad detectors. Unverified — flagged above. Separately measured: 6GEMSDOE's blocked-CV 0.1698 sits exactly on the random@3% floor (0.170), i.e. no held-out catalogue skill above chance.
